What Is Implicit Bias?

Implicit Bias operates subconsciously and involuntarily. Everyone can be susceptible to implicit bias. It is important to ask yourself questions, to try and uncover your own. Additionally, utilizing resources like the implicit bias test can help us to uncover, and unlearn implicit biases.

These conversations aren’t easy but are incredibly useful for sharpening the ability to be honest about how bias operates. Implicit bias is something that can be explored individually but its definition is straightforward.

Understanding the Implicit Bias Test, also called the Implicit Association Test, is a free online test from Harvard's Project Implicit. It measures how quickly you pair concepts, such as social groups and positive or negative words, to reveal associations you may not be aware of.

What Is Intersectionality?

Intersectionality is a way of analyzing the complexities of our social experience. Inequality is shaped by many interconnected factors, including, but not limited to, race, gender, class, and ability. Kimberlé Crenshaw uses an analogy to frame intersectionality. Imagine an intersection where each street represents a different social structure, like race and gender. The cars and traffic moving down those streets represent discriminatory policies, institutional practices, and biases. A Black woman standing at the crossroads can be hit by cars coming from both directions at once, experiencing racial and gender discrimination simultaneously.

We see intersectionality as a tool for asking where an institution’s categories break down. When systems treat race, gender, sexuality, and class as separate, the people at the overlap become invisible.
